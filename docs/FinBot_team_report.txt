0. THE SHORT VERSION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- FinBot is a finance chatbot for India that shows its working.
  - Every number comes from a calculator tool.
  - Every rule comes with an official source.
  - It refuses scams and risky advice.
- Two models run inside the same FinBot system:
  - The SMALL MODEL is Qwen3-8B, fine-tuned by us. It is a 5.9 GB file and runs offline on a 16 GB MacBook.
  - The BIG MODEL is Qwen3.6-27B, used as it comes. It needs a rented GPU.
- CA Foundation (97 ICAI Business Mathematics questions):
  - The small model scores 79-80%. The big model scores 95%.
  - ICAI's pass mark is 40% per paper; a distinction is 70%.
- Blind test (100 questions nobody looked at while building): small model 72, big model 90.
- Safety: the small model handled 26 out of 26 scam and risky-advice questions safely.
- Cost per 1,000 answers:
  - The small model costs Rs 5 on the laptop and Rs 81 on a rented GPU.
  - Claude Opus 5.5 would cost Rs 2,347 and GPT-5.6 Sol Rs 2,608 for the same work.
  - The cheapest cloud models (Claude Haiku 5.5 at Rs 70, GPT-5.6 Luna at Rs 140) cost about the same as our GPU.
- Speed: the small model typically answers in 12 s on a GPU and 66 s on the laptop. The big model takes 18 s.
- Build:
  - 16-21 Oct, with 99 commits and 548 automated tests.
  - The final training run took 4.0 h on one A100 (about Rs 750).
  - GPU rental for versions 4 and 5 was about $60, and about half of that was wasted on idle GPUs.
- Not yet tested: ChatGPT and Claude on the same questions. We compare them on PRICE ONLY.


1. WHAT FINBOT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

FinBot is the team's reference chatbot for the exhibition. It shows the design choices that
make a financial chatbot trustworthy. It does not claim that a small model is smarter than a
bank's chatbot (SPEC wording).

What it does:
  - Calculations: EMI, SIP, FD/RD, tax, capital gains, bonds, NPV/IRR, ratios and so on.
    All of them are done by 21 deterministic calculator tools, never by the model.
  - Rules and products: it searches 3,954 passages and cites them as [S1], [S2]. They come
    from 28 official documents (RBI, SEBI, PIB, NSI, IRDAI, AMFI, DICGC, NCFE, NPS and the
    Income Tax department) and 2 OpenStax textbooks.
  - Safety: it refuses stock tips, "guaranteed" returns, OTP or password requests, and
    anything it cannot verify.
  - Languages: English, Hindi, Tamil and Hinglish, with the same calculators underneath.

What it cannot do: see a customer's account. It does not check balances, block cards,
make payments or show a CIBIL score.


2. HOW IT ANSWERS A QUESTION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Your question
     -> Pre-check     turns "Rs 20 lakh" into 2,000,000; hides PAN and Aadhaar numbers;
                      picks the right kind of calculation
     -> Search        finds the 3 most relevant passages in the official documents
     -> Model plans   the 8B or 27B decides which calculator to call and with what numbers
     -> Calculator    21 exact tools return the numbers (and year-wise tables)
     -> Reviewer      checks that every figure in the draft came from a calculator result
                      and that phone numbers appear in a source
     -> Answer        with sources and working

If the reviewer finds a figure it can't trace, the model gets one rewrite. If that also
fails, the unverified part is removed or the answer is marked "not reliable".

Real example: an early version invented a fraud helpline number. The reviewer now blocks
any phone number that isn't in a source, and the bot gives 1930 from the government notice.


3. THE TWO MODELS, IN PLAIN WORDS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"8B" and "27B" mean 8 and 27 billion parameters, the learned numbers inside a model.
More parameters usually means better reasoning, but more memory and computing power.

  SMALL MODEL  Qwen3-8B (open weights, Apache 2.0), fine-tuned by us with LoRA.
               Q5_K_M file: 5.85 GB. Runs on the MacBook (M5, 16 GB) with Ollama, offline.
  BIG MODEL    Qwen3.6-27B (FP8), used as it comes. Its weights alone need about 28 GB of
               GPU memory, so it runs on a rented Lambda A100 40 GB with vLLM 0.31.0.

TEACHER AND STUDENT (how the small model was trained, also called "distillation"):
  1. We wrote practice questions where the small model was weak (455 in the last round).
  2. The big model (the "teacher") answered them inside the full FinBot system.
  3. Our code checked each answer against a correct value. 359 were kept and 96 were thrown away.
  4. The small model (the "student") was fine-tuned on all 2,189 checked examples.
     It learned the teacher's habits: which calculator to use, how to set out the working
     and when to refuse.


4. THE TEST SETS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Set                  Size  What it is
  -------------------  ----  -------------------------------------------------------------
  CA Foundation          97  ICAI Business Mathematics questions (BoS suggested answers and
                             question banks), marked against ICAI's official answers.
                             FinBot gets no answer options.
  Blind (fresh) set     100  New questions written by a separate helper after v4. Nobody
                             reads them, and nothing is tuned on them. This is the honest score.
  Test bank             159  Our own mixed set: Indian and textbook maths, concepts, rules,
                             safety and languages.
  McGraw-Hill            34  Course practice problems (184 numeric parts), graded at 1%.
                             Private: never committed, never used for training.
  Held-out calcs         20  Calculation questions kept out of all training.
  Team question bank     83  The team's questions. 11 have a checkable answer.

RULES (please keep to them):
  - All of these are TEST ONLY. Never train on them, and never paste them into training data.
  - Training data is checked against the test sets for near-copies before each fine-tune.
  - Never read the blind set's questions or tune anything on them, or it stops being blind.
  - Never commit books, PDFs, kb/raw, kb/index, .env, passwords or fine-tune data.
  - Push only to the mohammedr3za GitHub.
  - CAVEAT: the v4.1 code fixes were found from test-bank and CA failures, so those two
    scores are optimistic. Quote the blind set when someone asks "how good is it really".


5. RESULTS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 CA Foundation, 97 ICAI questions (pass 40%, distinction 70%)

  Setup                                   Score         Median time
  --------------------------------------  ------------  -----------
  Small model v3, GPU                     72/97  (74%)  15 s
  Small model v4, GPU                     76/97  (78%)  21 s
  Small model v4 + v4.1 code fixes, GPU   78/97  (80%)  20 s
  Small model v5, Ollama on GPU           69/97  (71%)  22 s
  Small model v5, vLLM on GPU             77/97  (79%)  12 s
  Small model v4, MacBook, offline        78/97  (80%)  see 6
  Big model 27B, GPU                      92/97  (95%)  20 s

  - The laptop scores exactly the same as the same model on a GPU. Running offline costs
    speed, not accuracy.
  - Why v5 dropped on Ollama: it skipped tools more often and gave 13 blank fallbacks.
    Served on vLLM with forced tool calls, it had only 2.
  - The small model's 20 misses (v5, vLLM):
      6  a calculator call failed (bad arguments)
      6  the calculator ran but the method or the numbers were wrong
      5  its own reviewer blocked unverified figures ("not sure" instead of a guess)
      3  it never called the calculator
  - Example the poster uses: "In how many years will money double at 5% compound interest?"
    ICAI's multiple-choice key says 14.3 years, which is the nearest option. FinBot's
    calculator says 14.21 years (exact: ln 2 / ln 1.05 = 14.2067).

6. SPEED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Timed on the same 37 CA questions (the ones the laptop answered while awake and plugged in):

  Setup                                    Typical (median)   9 in 10 within
  ---------------------------------------  ----------------   --------------
  Small model, A100 GPU (~10 at a time)          12 s               24 s
  Big model, A100 GPU                            18 s               47 s
  Small model, MacBook M5 (one at a time)        66 s              143 s

  - CORRECTION: the laptop's first 60 CA answers ran while the Mac was asleep with its lid
    closed, on battery. That inflated their times (the median over all 97 was 74 s), but not
    their marks. See lesson 5 in section 8.
  - Why FinBot is slower than ChatGPT's first words: it doesn't stream a guess. It plans,
    calls the calculator, checks every figure and rewrites once if a check fails. Each
    answer takes 2 model calls, or 4 for heavy ones.
  - GPU throughput (measured): small model 2,330 answers an hour, big model 766 an hour.

  What it means:
  - Opus 5.5 and GPT-5.6 Sol cost about 30x our small model on a rented GPU.
  - The cheapest cloud models (Haiku 5.5 and GPT-5.6 Luna) cost about the same as renting
    a GPU. FinBot's real advantages are that it can run on hardware you already own
    (Rs 5 per 1,000 on the laptop) and that the question never leaves the room.
  - Cloud prices above INCLUDE prompt caching. Without it, Opus 5.5 or GPT-5.6 Sol would
    cost about Rs 10,700 per 1,000 answers.
  - This is price only. ChatGPT's and Claude's ACCURACY on our questions was not tested.

7.2 Subscriptions (India, per month)
  ChatGPT Go Rs 399 · ChatGPT Plus Rs 1,999 · Claude Pro about Rs 2,000 (annual billing)
  - One month of ChatGPT Plus = 10.6 hours of the A100 = about 25,000 FinBot-8B answers.
  - An 8-hour exhibition day on the A100 costs about Rs 1,500, however many visitors come.

7.3 At a bank's scale (estimates from docs/cost_and_value.md)
  - An 8B on Indian GPUs costs about Rs 0.08-0.10 per answer.
  - A call-centre call costs about Rs 45-55 (a vendor estimate; no official Indian figure).
  - Including staff, redundancy and audits, a finance helper in SBI's YONO app would cost
    about Rs 2.4-3.3 per answer. It pays for itself if about 1 answer in 15-20 replaces a
    phone call.
  - Indian rules favour self-hosting:
    - RBI's payment-data storage rule (2018) requires payment data to stay in India.
    - The DPDP Act 2023 can restrict sending personal data abroad.
    - RBI's FREE-AI report (2025) asks that sensitive data stay "under the control of the
      institution".
  - That document's throughput figures were estimates. We have since measured 2,330
    answers an hour for the 8B on an A100.

7.4 How the cost numbers were worked out
  - Token use (from the vLLM server log of 356 answers):
    - 25,909 input tokens per answer, 92.2% of them repeated instructions served from cache;
      2,021 new tokens per answer.
    - 499 output tokens per answer.
  - Cloud cost = new tokens x input price + repeated tokens x cache price + output x output
    price. Claude's new input is billed at the 5-minute cache-write price, 1.25x its input
    price.
  - Prices are list prices on 21 Oct 2026 (platform.claude.com and developers.openai.com):

      Model              Input   Cache hit   Output   (US$ per million tokens)
      Claude Haiku 5.5   0.10    0.01        0.50
      Claude Sonnet 5.5  2.00    0.10        10
      Claude Opus 5.5    4.00    0.20        20
      GPT-5.6 Luna       0.20    0.02        1.20
      GPT-5.6 Sol        4.00    0.40        20

  - Rented GPU: Lambda A100 40 GB at $1.99 an hour, divided by the measured answers per hour.
  - Laptop: about 35 W while answering (measured with ioreg; 26-39 W), times the 66 s median,
    at a household tariff of Rs 7/kWh.
  - Exchange rate: Rs 94.4 per US$ (Wise mid-market, 21 Oct 2026).


8. HOW WE BUILT IT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Timeline
  16 Oct  Spec approved; calculator tools, safe maths engine, search over official documents.
  17 Oct  v2 and v3 fine-tunes (first teacher data from the 27B). v3: CA 74%, McGraw 20/34.
  18 Oct  v4: more teacher data aimed at v3's gaps. Test bank 84%, CA 78%, McGraw 19/34.
  19 Oct  v4.1: code fixes only, same model. New routes for flat-rate loans, emergency funds,
          post-tax real returns and "can I still claim"; the helpline check; fraud search
          terms; 4 new official documents (PIB 1930, NSI SCSS/PPF, SEBI F&O study).
          Test bank 89%, CA 80%.
  19-20   Blind set written. v5: 455 new practice questions, 359 kept; 2,189 examples in total;
          trained 4.0 h on one A100. On vLLM: test bank 91%, CA 79%, blind 72/100.
  21 Oct  Big model on the blind set: 90/100. Laptop CA run: 78/97. Poster built.

Training run (v5)
  Base Qwen3-8B · LoRA rank 32 · max sequence length 12,288 · 2 epochs · eval loss 0.34
  · 14,440 s (4.0 h) on one A100 40 GB · exported to a 5.85 GB Q5_K_M GGUF for Ollama.

GPU spend (from the run logs; Lambda has no billing API)
  v4:          teacher $20 (about $17 of it idle) + train and eval $8.5 + retest $2.7  = about $31
  v5:          failed attempt $5 + teacher $4.3 + train and eval $16.3 (about $6 stalled) = about $26
  Blind test:  big model on an A100, $3 (about $2.2 idle)
  Total:       about $60 (Rs 5,600), of which about $30 paid for idle GPUs.
  (Earlier rounds and exhibition serving are not included.)

Lessons learned (please follow these for any GPU run)
  1. Every rented GPU must shut itself down: at the end, on failure, and from a watchdog
     timer. One idle teacher box burned 9 hours.
  2. Never hold one SSH session open through a long remote step. Run it detached on the box,
     write status markers, and poll with short SSH calls.
  3. Pin vLLM 0.31.0. Version 0.32.0 decodes the 27B about 5x slower on an A100.
  4. On a 40 GB GPU, don't load two 8B models in Ollama with 8 parallel slots each; they
     thrash and hang. Use 4 slots.
  5. Keep the MacBook on its charger during unattended runs. With the lid closed on battery
     it sleeps even under caffeinate. The monitor and watchdog then stall while the GPU
     keeps billing.


9. WHERE IT STILL FAILS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Rules: 7 of 12 (small) and 8 of 12 (big) on the blind set. Rules change, and the bot only
    knows the documents it was given.
  - Harder maths: the small model gets about 1 CA question in 5 wrong (see the misses in 5.1).
  - It is slow on a laptop: over a minute per answer.
  - Our tests are not the official exam: 97 practice questions from one paper, open answer.
  - It cannot see accounts or do transactions.
  - The CA questions come from ICAI's public material, which big models may have seen in
    pretraining. That applies to the 27B and to ChatGPT/Claude as much as to us. Our own
    fine-tuning data was checked for overlap.


10. WHAT WE CAN AND CANNOT CLAIM
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  SAY   "79-80% on 97 ICAI CA Foundation Business Mathematics practice questions"
  NOT   "FinBot passed the CA exam" (one paper's practice questions, not the four-paper exam)

  SAY   "Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-5.6 Sol would cost about 30x more for the same work"
  NOT   "better than ChatGPT" (their accuracy on our questions was not measured)

  SAY   "as cheap as the cheapest cloud AI, and your data stays with you"
  NOT   "cheaper than any AI" (Claude Haiku 5.5 costs about the same as our rented GPU)

  SAY   "the small model is as safe as the big one on our scam questions"
  NOT   "it never makes mistakes"
